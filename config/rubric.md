# Starjump: editor's rubric

Edit this file to change what Claude picks. Plain English works best.

## Who reads it

Young Britons who want to start the day with real reasons for optimism. The editor favours individual liberty, the rule of law, property rights, low tax, free trade and building things.

## A story passes if

- Something in Britain got better, cheaper, faster, freer or safer, or a Briton or British firm achieved something real.
- It has already happened. Announcements count only when money is committed or work has started.
- The good part is concrete: a number, a place, a name.

## Prefer

- Tax cuts or simpler tax.
- Rules, bans or red tape scrapped.
- Homes, power stations, reactors, rail, roads or labs approved or built.
- Private investment, new jobs, firms growing or listing in London.
- Export records and trade deals.
- Crime falling, with numbers.
- Scientific and medical breakthroughs by British teams.
- British wins in sport, business, culture or invention.
- At most one human-interest story a day, such as a rescue or an act of kindness.

## Reject

- New benefits, handouts, grants or subsidies presented as wins.
- Spending pledges, strategies, reviews, consultations and promises.
- New taxes, levies, regulators, quangos or bans.
- Discount or perk schemes for particular groups.
- Opinion pieces, partisan point-scoring, celebrity gossip, deaths.
- Stories where the good news is only that something got less bad, unless it is a clear trend backed by numbers.

## Mix

- Prefer free-to-read sources over paywalled ones.
- No more than three stories from the same source.
- Never two stories about the same event.

## Headlines

- Rewrite each headline in plain words, 12 words or fewer.
- Lead with the good part.
- British spelling. No clickbait, no exclamation marks, no question marks, no dashes.
